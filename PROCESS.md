# Process

## Starting Point

I wrote this essay in response to the question “Why are we here?” from my
perspective as a game developer and technical artist. My main argument is that
learning programming still matters when AI can generate code, because people
must understand systems, evaluate risks, and build tools for their own creative
and production needs.

## AI Tools Used

I used ChatGPT to brainstorm possible arguments, organize the essay structure,
and improve the clarity of some sentences. I also used it to question whether
my examples supported the main argument. I wrote the personal experiences,
including my Tencent internship, retrieval-augmented generation project, and
handwritten-image recognition experiment.

## What I Kept

I kept the distinction between generating code and understanding or judging
code. I also kept the examples about outdated project documentation, checking
game materials, and using code to understand AI. These examples connect the
abstract argument to my experience and explain why programming gives me agency
as a technical artist.

## What I Rejected

I rejected general suggestions that said people should learn programming only
because technology is the future. That argument was vague and did not reflect
my position. I also removed claims that treated AI as either completely
reliable or completely harmful. The final essay presents AI as useful, but
something whose output still requires human understanding and verification.

## Revision and Verification

I revised the essay for a clear progression: the changing role of programming,
the risks of AI in production, my practical examples, and the creative value of
code. I checked the word count, added the reference to Dylan Beattie's *The Art
of Code*, and reviewed the final text for accuracy and clarity.

## Final Responsibility

I made the final decisions about the argument, examples, wording, and source.
The submitted essay represents my own position. AI assisted with brainstorming
and editing, but I reviewed the result and remain responsible for its content.
